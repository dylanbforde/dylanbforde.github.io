01Education / game theory

Tragedy of the Correlated Commons

When individually sensible choices collapse a society’s search strategy.

Prestige-oriented educational choices can be locally rational while making everyone explore the same narrow region. The loss is not only diversity as an aesthetic good; it is reduced coverage of the possibility space from which new expertise, institutions, and discoveries emerge.

Thesis

A population can over-optimize for legible, low-variance outcomes and underinvest in unusual depth-first paths.

Mechanism

Shared status signals correlate family strategies. Correlation crowds known peaks and removes the portfolio benefit of independent search.

Open question

Which institutions can protect exploration without asking individuals to absorb all of its risk?

02Economics / optimization

The Communist Constraint

Central planning as a computational bottleneck.

The essay reframes the coordination problem in optimization language. When local conditions are high-dimensional and continuously changing, the difficulty is not simply finding well-intentioned planners; it is collecting fresh information, computing an update, and applying it before the world changes again.

Thesis

Some economic limits are best understood as constraints on information bandwidth, latency, and objective specification.

Mechanism

Distributed actors produce noisy but frequent local updates. A central optimizer receives a cleaner-looking batch only after delay and compression.

Open question

Where can modern computation improve coordination, and where does it merely move the bottleneck?

  1. The optimization analogy
  2. Information and dimensionality
  3. Stale gradients

03Genetics / long horizon

Compounding Returns of Genetic Capital

Small changes become different questions when they persist across generations.

Many technologies are evaluated through their immediate effect. Germline interventions force a longer accounting: a small directional change can propagate, interact with assortative choice and institutions, and alter the starting point of subsequent generations.

Thesis

Intergenerational persistence makes the total effect of selection qualitatively different from a one-time intervention.

Mechanism

Heritable changes alter the base on which later choices act; even modest increments can accumulate over a long horizon.

Open question

How should governance weigh distant compounding, uneven access, uncertainty, and individual autonomy?

  1. From capital to inheritance
  2. The compounding model
  3. Technology and selection
  4. Governance under long horizons
