01Education / game theory

Tragedy of the Correlated Commons

What happens when sensible choices send everyone down the same path?

Choosing a prestigious education can make sense for a family. If enough families make the same choice, though, fewer people explore other possibilities. That leaves less room to find new kinds of expertise, institutions, and discoveries.

Thesis

People can put too much into safe, recognizable outcomes and too little into following an unusual interest in depth.

Mechanism

Families respond to the same signs of status and end up pursuing the same opportunities. Society loses the benefit of having people try different things.

Open question

Which institutions can protect exploration without asking individuals to absorb all of its risk?

02Economics / optimization

The Communist Constraint

Can a central plan keep up with the economy?

I look at economic coordination as an optimization problem. A planner has to collect information about many changing local conditions, calculate an update, and apply it before those conditions change again. Good intentions alone cannot solve that problem.

Thesis

Some economic limits make more sense when we consider how much information can be gathered, how long it takes to arrive, and how precisely the objective can be defined.

Mechanism

People make frequent local adjustments using imperfect information. A central optimizer gets a batch that looks cleaner, but the information has been compressed and takes time to arrive.

Open question

Where can modern computation improve coordination, and where does it merely move the bottleneck?

03Genetics / long horizon

Compounding Returns of Genetic Capital

How should we think about changes that last for generations?

We often judge a technology by its immediate effects. Germline interventions require us to look further ahead. A small directional change can be inherited, interact with assortative choice and institutions, and change the starting point for later generations.

Thesis

Selection has a different kind of total effect when its consequences persist across generations, rather than ending with a one-time intervention.

Mechanism

An inherited change affects the starting point for later choices. Even small increments can accumulate over a long time.

Open question

How should governance weigh distant compounding, uneven access, uncertainty, and individual autonomy?

  1. From capital to inheritance
  2. The compounding model
  3. Technology and selection
  4. Governance under long horizons
